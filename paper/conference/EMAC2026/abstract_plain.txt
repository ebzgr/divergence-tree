Marketing interventions are increasingly designed through experimentation and personalization that optimize firm objectives, yet firms face tradeoffs across outcomes and horizons. Existing personalization emphasizes who should receive an intervention more than why tradeoffs arise across customers. We propose a descriptive framework for heterogeneous tradeoffs: we extend recursive partitioning to two outcomes and build interpretable segments from standard A/B data, surfacing groups with different joint responses---including localized segments where one outcome improves while another worsens. The output supports organizational learning, redesign, and surfacing harms hidden in opaque targeting. Simulations show strong detection of localized contradictions in large, noisy panels with parsimonious trees versus a two-step benchmark that estimates each outcome separately and merges segment labels. We note implications for responsible personalization and accountability.
